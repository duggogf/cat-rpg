# Paper Playtest — Round 1 (whole game, all first-draft docs)

*Reviewer: qa-lead · Date: 2026-11-23 · Method: the rules as written, played in my head by three players through six windows. I ran no build and did no simulation. Every timing here is arithmetic on the docs' own numbers.*

**Docs played:** `creative-brief.md`, `game-pillars.md` and `game-concept.md` (read in full); `combat-system.md`, `nemesis-system.md`, `territory-management.md`, `crew-and-progression.md`, `dual-story-progression.md`, `hud.md` and `key-screens.md` (read by section).

**Sections not read** (required by the protocol): combat's F-1 to F-9 formula bodies, Tuning Knobs, Game Feel and Acceptance Criteria; nemesis R3.2–R3.9 generation tables, R6.2–R6.7 and F1–F18 bodies; territory §4.8–4.12, §7–13; crew's Move and Knack catalogue rows (except Home Moves), F1–F6, F9–F11 and Tuning; narrative Lives 3–7 beat sheets, §8, §11–13 and §16–21; most of hud.md (§1–12); key-screens §2–7 and §9–13. Nothing below depends on a table I did not read. Where I relied on a heading or one row, I say so.

**Verdict on the design as a whole: MAJOR REVISION NEEDED.** Each doc on its own is strong, and two of them (nemesis, narrative) are excellent. But nobody owns the day as a whole, so the docs contradict each other at the three moments that matter most: the first 30 minutes, the daily loop and Life 8.

## 1. Window 1 — the first 30 minutes

There are three different first halves-hour on paper, one each from UX, Narrative and Combat. I played the UX beat sheet (key-screens §8.3) because it is the only one with minute marks, and logged every point where another doc disagrees.

**0:00–0:03 (all players).** The cold open, the box in the rain (narrative K1.1). Mio is hooked by the yellow raincoat, the face bending into frame and the hummed three notes. Aki is patient. At 0:01, UX puts the player in the house on the "first morning". Narrative's next beat, though, is K1.2 *Gran Says No*, an **arrival** beat on day 1: the Box is left below the step, the kitten not yet inside. UX's 0:02 has **the Call sung from the veranda, granting the first Bond heart**. Narrative reserves the first Call for K1.3, gated `T1 or day 2`, the Dusk half of "The Bell and the First Call", and builds its emotional weight on it being the first. **Collision PP-01.** At 0:02 there is also no answer to whether the day clock runs in the garden: the brief pauses it "inside Haru's House", and the garden is inside the safe wall but outside the house (**gap PP-02**).

**0:03–0:06.** A crow on the lane wall: Swat and dodge. Aki gets a light string with hit-stop on a 0.5× Nerve Critter. That is fine for 3 minutes and thin for 3 minutes more.

**0:06.** The first Paw fills and Slow Blink opens itself with one Move in it. This is good onboarding. Aki notices it is a one-item menu.

**0:08–0:10.** An "older cat on the wall" says "Use your whiskers, Collar." Mio loves it. Narrative has not authored this character (UX OQ24). Then Whisker Sense, and **Spray the lamp post at 0:10, "a Patch can be yours"**. Under territory §3.4, a rival Patch is taken by erosion, Bristle, 1–2 turf waves, a Standoff with the Holder, a Verdict and then a Claim Spray. A Spray at 0:10 can only be a Counter-Spray (−30 Scent, loud, starts the waves) or a scripted exception that nobody has specified (**PP-03**).

**0:12.** Frazzle and **Pile-On on a single Stray**: "solo Pile-On is Mikan alone in the dust cloud — assumption". Combat CR-12.1 makes a Pile-On unavailable unless **at least two Posse cats** are standing, edge case 7 says "a pile of one is not the signature image", and CR-21 introduces Frazzle and Pile-On in **Life 2** with the first Crew cat. **Collision PP-04.** This is the signature image of the game, and the docs disagree on whether it exists in hour 1.

**0:15–0:17.** A Sunspot nap, then the first Rival Standoff ("Collar!"). Nemesis R13.1 says only the first Patch's Keeper and 2 Roamers are active in Life 1, so the first Rival is almost certainly the **First Thorn**, who is scripted to **bolt at 25% Nerve with no Verdict** (nemesis R12.2). UX promises the **first Verdict at 0:20**. **Collision PP-05.** P3 Ren, who is watching for this, sees the first Rival fight end in a forced flee and reads it as the system cheating, which nemesis OQ5 already fears. If Aki is rough and Routs it from above 25% in one burst, the flee is skipped (nemesis edge case) and the Verdict appears. The same game teaches two different lessons depending on the player's damage output.

**0:17 alternative: Mio loses.** That is canon. Mikan slinks home, the **day ends at minute 17**, 20% of carried Morsels are lost, the Rival is promoted and Gloats, Crew Loyalty takes −3 (there is no Crew yet) and Clout takes −5. A cosy player who has not yet seen the evening vignette is sent to bed for losing the tutorial (UX OQ23 flags this; nobody has fixed it). **PP-06.**

**0:20–0:24.** First Verdict, if it happens. The prompt shows four options plus, if Recruit is chosen, an Offering ladder paid from **carried** Morsels, a willingness roll and possible refusal. A 20-minute-old player is being taught nemesis, crew and economy rules at once (**PP-07**, see §9). Then Dusk and the Call (its second "first" time), the evening vignette, sleep. Mio is happy here: this is the game she bought.

**0:27–0:32.** Morning: the Doorstep, the first small fish pile, a 2–3 line Morning Sniff and the Fence Line reveal. Ren is delighted. Daifuku is visible on the gatepost and the hierarchy reads at once.

**0:32.** "First recruit arrives at the Lot" in Life 1. Crew reserves a story recruit in Life 1, Combat's CR-21 says Life 2, and the brief says the Posse "grows one cat at a time across Lives 1–2". This one is reconcilable, but nobody has reconciled it (**PP-08**).

**30-minute verdict per player**
- **Aki (P1):** 30 minutes, one cat, about 4 fights (two crows, a Stray, possibly one Rival), one Move, no party. The FF7-plus-Pokémon promise does not appear in the first session. Conducting, the reason Aki came, starts at 0:35 under UX, or in Life 2 under Combat. If Life 1 is narrative's 4 days, Life 2 starts around 1:45–2:00. **PP-09 (BLOCKER for the pitch).**
- **Mio (P2):** the best first session of the three. Two home scenes and the Call. At risk if she loses at 0:17.
- **Ren (P3):** intrigued by the Fence Line, irritated by the First Thorn script, and sees no economy yet.

**Life 1 length is undefined across the docs (PP-10):** UX ends Life 1 at **0:50**; narrative's target is 3–4 days (F7 budget: 4 days ≈ 2 h at 30 min a day); territory's reference player takes 3 days; crew expects Lv 3 at Life 1's end. Life 1's goal is "first Patch held" (T2), and the tutorial Patch is Low Wall Corner, so a competent player can meet it on **day 1**. That fires narrative's **catch-up rule** (§4.4 rule 5) inside the tutorial: K1.3 *The Bell and the First Call*, the beat that teaches the game's central motif, becomes an "essential cut" or a montage insert. The fastest players get the thinnest version of the most important vocabulary lesson in the game.

## 3. Window 3 — hours 2–4, the first district (Life 2, Hidamari Lane)

The cat flap arrives at K2.3 (`T2 or day 5`). Night opens. The day is now 20 minutes of clock.

**A typical post-flap day for Ren:** Morning Sniff with up to 5 items (a raid forecast, a Wavering warning, gossip). The Fence Line animates up to 5 changes. Lot: Lessons (each cat gives one and takes one per day), **Share a Meal once per cat per day** (+3 Loyalty, the largest single Loyalty input), Rations, posting. Town: Spray every own post daily (once per post per day), Rub every Rub Mark daily (+5 each), visit Feeders (once a day each), Back Door scraps (once a day each), 3 Caches per district per day, **Share a Morsel with each Humbled resident** (once per visit, −8 Grudge), a Friend Stray Trust pip (once a day), Spats to watch, a Clean Duel call-out to attend within 2 days. Then Night for Clout, plus a Caterwaul.

Every one of these is a daily-reset, once-per-day action. Taken together they are the "open world of checklists" the anti-pillars forbid, assembled by four authors who could each see only their own two or three items (**PP-13, BLOCKER**). Ren will do all of them, because each one is the optimal move. Mio will feel she is "doing it wrong" by skipping them. Aki will skip them and then be told by the Morning Sniff that Loyalty is falling.

**Daifuku (end of Life 2).** Aki finally gets the set piece: knocked off his wall, Splat, Ruffled, phases, Full Pile-On, Zoomies. Combat's own model says fights run short (F-10: the Boss lands at 2.3–3.5 minutes against a 3–5 minute target). That is fine for Aki. Then the Den Verdict: the Boss plus up to 2 Captains (nemesis R8.7 escorts) means **up to three sequential Verdict prompts**, each possibly with an Offering and a roll (**PP-14**).

**The Style chart lies to Ren.** Crew §3.13 builds its Boss rhythm on a matchup table ("Daifuku wants a Yowler"; "**Ikari resists Mikan's whole native kit**"). Combat's CR-14.2, which owns the chart, has **Bruiser resists Claw** (Crew: Thump), **Scrapper resists Fang** (Crew: Claw) and **Trickster no weakness** (Crew: resists Thump, weak to Fang). Under Combat's rules, Ikari does not resist Claw at all. Ren builds the "counter Posse" the Crew doc recommends and it is wrong (**PP-15**).

**Mio at hours 2–4:** Life 2 Keystones are lovely: the study lamp, the flap, exam nerves (the first Comfort), the envelope. The pre-flap structure "trains the habit". Her risk is the Daifuku requirement. Narrative Rule 7 says **"the human story never advances past the Plateau without the turf goal"**, so Haru's envelope and every later chapter are **locked behind Routing a 3–5 minute phased Boss**. Kitten mode (enemy damage ×0.6, Nerve ×0.8) plus Auto-Scrap is the only help. No doc gives a story-first player a guaranteed way through a Boss (**PP-16, MAJOR**). The pillars say combat is central, so this may be accepted, but it should be a ruling, not an accident.

State per territory §5.4: three districts in play, about 13 Crew, about 7 Sentries, Tribute about 225–300, first Established upgrade, "raids on the Arcade while you are on the Hill".

**Ren's mid-game day, minute by minute** (my estimate from the docs' numbers):

| Real min | Activity | Clock |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1.5 | Morning slot (Texture or Keystone), Feeding, Goodbye | paused |
| 1.5–4 | Doorstep: Tribute, 5-item Sniff, "last night" replay on the Fence Line (5 animations) | paused |
| 4–12 | Lot: **13 Share-a-Meals** (one each), up to 13 Lessons with pending replace prompts, Rations, re-posting Routed Sentries, a Patch upgrade, Posse build against Oboro's Prowler kit | paused |
| 12–? | Walk Hidamari → Tsukimi (the assumed adjacency puts Tsukimi next to Hidamari). Keep `seen` alive in **Arcade** too (2-day grace, then −15% a day), which means a detour the other way. Spray, Rub, fight. Caterwaul in the Arcade while on the Hill: a 120 s window, no fast travel (territory OQ14 admits it may be unreachable) | 20 min |
| … | Night on Tsukimi Hill, "comes alive at night"; Lantern Eyes is night-centred; **Oboro is seen only after dusk** | — |
| end | Late arrival | paused |

At roughly 9 encounters a day (crew's A-7 assumption) of 1–1.5 minutes, plus Verdicts, plus about 8 minutes of Lot, plus about 4 minutes of home, plus 20 minutes of clock, **a mid-game day takes 45–55 real minutes, not the brief's 30** (**PP-17, MAJOR**). The concept's 30–60 minute weeknight player gets one day per sitting, and that day ends in a Night district.

**The Life 4 collision (PP-18, MAJOR):** the Life 4 district, Tsukimi, is the night district, and its Boss appears only after dusk. The player who claims it does so at Night and comes home **Late**. Narrative's Closing Keystone for Life 4, K4.5 *The Small Box*, the proposal, is staged in §12.2 as a **Witness** scene. Rule 6 says a Closing plays on whatever arrival follows T=4, so on a Late arrival the player gets the **Forensic** version of the proposal. The game's district design pushes the player out at Night exactly when the story's most joyful scene needs them home at Dusk. Tsukimi is Life 4 in the canon table, so the brief itself causes this.

**The curfew trade, as each player feels it at hour 11:**
- *Home by Dusk* gives Bond (about 1 point per day toward hearts) and Well-Fed of **+2% max Nerve per heart** the next day, so +10–16% at this stage. It costs a quarter of the clock, the Night Rivals, Night Clout (capped at +1 per Rout, max 5 a day) and Night Shinies.
- *Out all Night* gives Clout and Shinies, and the Bond floor lifts the night-owl to 3 hearts anyway (narrative F3), so their Well-Fed would be +6% on the evenings they do come home.
- Ren concludes home-by-Dusk earns **more Morsels per real minute** (territory §5.5 strategy B) while Night earns the gated items. The real driver is which district the Life sends him to. In Life 4 that means Night. **The trade is decided by the brief's district order rather than by the player each evening (PP-19).** Mio goes home every night and is fine, until Life 4's Boss demands Night.

**Aki at hour 11:** a full Posse at last, Mikan with a Repertoire of borrowed capstones, 3 Paws from Life 4 and Scrapper Bite 1.20. Crew OQ4 already suspects **Mikan is the best damage dealer in every Posse**. Add combat's AI cadence (0.75 MV/s against the player's 1.5–2.1) and Aki plays Mikan almost all the time. He switches only for Bap and Hair's-Breadth, which only the controlled cat can do, so switching away from Mikan *loses* those as well. "Conducting four cats" becomes "playing Mikan with three buff-and-heal bots" (**PP-20, MAJOR**). The control set is 15+ verbs (combat OQ3), and Aki likes that. Mio is drowning in it and turns on Auto-Scrap.

**Ren at hour 11 finds three exploits or dead ends:**
1. **Neglect-driven betrayal never fires** (crew OQ8): there are enough posts that nobody idles, and he can keep every ex-Captain posted. Nemesis's Turncoat drama depends on Loyalty below 30. With daily Share-a-Meal at +3, no cat he feeds ever Wavers. Betrayal turns into a tax for not doing the chore.
2. **The recruit gate has three owners:** nemesis R7.2 (Grudge after the ledger below 90, or Regard ≥ 50, plus "Economy's offering paid"), crew §3.4 (a willingness roll against an Offering, blocked if more than 5 levels above Mikan) and the UX prompt (ears, plus a number). On one screen he sees a deterministic gate and a dice roll, and one of them can refuse him after the other said yes (**PP-21**).
3. **The same field name means two things:** `temperament` is nemesis's 6 personalities (Braggart, Brooder…) that drive barks and Gloats, and crew's 11 stat nudges (Stubborn, Bitey…) derived from `coat_seed`. A cat can be a "Fraidy" by bark and "Bitey" by stats. "Stubborn" is also a combat Knack. "Settled" is both a Humbled end-state and a Patch upgrade. "Lookout" is both a Sentry role and a Specialisation. "Pounce" is a combat verb, the gauge's name and a Crew Move. "Hiss" is a universal combat action and a Crew Yowler Move. "Bunny-Kick" is a combat basic attack (hold Whack) and a Crew Move (**PP-22, MAJOR data-model collision**). Crew and Combat have also each written their own starter Move list with different names (combat CR-17.3: Claw Flurry, Shred, Wall-Kick…; crew: Quick Swat, Bunny-Kick, Pounce, Whirl…).

**Mio at hour 11** has the best of the game: the lap war with Sora, his finger at nose height, Gran slowing. The Lot routine is the first thing she resents.

## 7. Where the day clock, the home visit and the open-world loop fight

1. **Nobody owns the way home.** Pillar 2's design test promises "we make the trip short and the arrival rewarding". No doc defines traversal speed, a route home, a shortcut or fast travel, and territory OQ14 says none exists. Home is mandatory twice a day, Caterwauls give 120 s, `seen` decays after 2 days per district, and Life 8 asks for Fresh Catch from 3 different districts. From Life 5 (Harbour) on, the commute decides the day (**PP-30, BLOCKER**).
2. **`rounds` is a time rule** (territory OQ4). Tribute scales with roaming minutes up to 10, which brushes against "not timed".
3. **Saves only at naps and at bedtime** (UX OQ15). On a handheld, a mid-phase interruption costs progress. The brief's platform target needs a suspend-on-quit.
4. **Morning slot, then Doorstep, then Lot, then town** is 4–12 real minutes of paused-clock management before play starts in the mid-game. That is the opposite of the pillars' "Opening 0–3 min: warmth, curiosity".
5. **Curl Up at home = Sunspot** (narrative's proposal) plus Sunspot naps costing half a phase: the safest heal is going home, which helps Pillar 2. But Routed Crew stay Routed until a nap, and combat has no in-fight revival, so a bad fight in Morning forces a half-phase nap or a trip home. Attrition is unmodelled (combat OQ6). This could make home a chore, or make it the rest stop the pillar wants. It is untested.
6. **The Posse loophole** (UX OQ9) against crew's Tail-Up: UX's PosseBuilder is "editable anywhere outside combat" with no travel, while crew's Tail-Up has the cat physically arrive over 5 s with a 30 s cooldown, and Sentries can't be Tail-Upped. These are two different rules for the same action (**PP-31**).

## 8. Where the cat fantasy or the tone breaks

- **The Turf screen and the economy.** Rations, upkeep, Skim, `seen`, `rounds`, Specialisations, Clout points next to milestones, and Power numbers in the Sentry picker add up to the spreadsheet Pillar 1 and the "wall, not a table" rule warn against (territory OQ8 and OQ15, UX OQ10). Cats do not budget rations.
- **"Carried Morsels"** has no cat-true form until Life 8 (territory assumption 11). Mikan pockets 400 fish.
- **Recruit willingness shown "as a number in system UI"** (crew §3.4) beside the ear posture. The number defeats the ears.
- **Life 8 Morning Sniff "allowed to be grim"** (nemesis) against the brief's "never make a street scene tragic" and narrative's "indifferent".
- **Jaunty street combat during a wake** (narrative OQ8). Audio's "thinner, slower" request is a hope, not a design.
- **Tutorial cat "Use your whiskers, Collar."** is charming, but it is a character nobody owns.

## 10. The five biggest design risks, ranked

1. **The day does not fit its own clock (PP-13, PP-17, PP-30).** Four authors each added "a few" once-per-day actions, and nobody owns traversal. The result is a 45–55 minute checklist day with a commute, which breaks the 30-minute session, the "not a checklist" anti-pillar and Pillar 2's "short trip, rewarding arrival". It is the risk every player hits every day, from hour 3 to the end.
2. **Life 8 is four incompatible designs and, as summed, a punishment for grief (PP-23, PP-24, PP-25).** The USP's centre has no single owner. The combined rules make the saddest chapter the hardest and most management-heavy, and its required act can stall for days.
3. **The conducting fantasy may not occur (PP-04, PP-09, PP-20).** The party arrives in hour 2. Mikan is the strongest cat, the controlled-only verbs anchor play on Mikan, AI cats deal a third of the player's output, and 15+ verbs are asked of the player. Combat is a headline system and the Pile-On is on the never-cut list, yet neither is in the first session.
4. **Story and street fight over the same evening (PP-16, PP-18, PP-19, PP-10).** Bosses hard-gate the home story, the night district forces the proposal into its Forensic version, the fastest players get catch-up cuts, and the curfew trade is lopsided or decided by the map. The USP's "two lives, one clock" turns out to be one life waiting on the other.
5. **Scope plus data-model collisions (PP-22, PP-15, PP-21; §12).** The content is unshippable as written, and the docs disagree on shared types (`temperament`, Style chart, Move catalogue, recruit gate). That means rework before any story can be implemented.

Close behind: nemesis legibility at a 55° camera (Scars on 0.5 m cats; nemesis OQ7), and the WB nemesis patent (nemesis Appendix B, blocking for public showing).

## 11. One game, or four systems?

**Right now it is four systems that share a schema and an event bus.** The contracts are good: `CatRouted`, `VerdictApplied`, `RaidRequested`/`RaidResolved` and the One Cat Schema really do let a Rival become Crew, a Sentry hold a grudge and Tribute pile on the Doorstep. But each doc optimised its own loop, and the places where they meet are the places where the player's experience was not designed:

- **The Verdict** is where nemesis, crew and economy each added a gate (PP-07, PP-14, PP-21).
- **The day** is where territory's maintenance, crew's per-cat upkeep, nemesis's daily interactions and narrative's two slots stack up without a budget (PP-13, PP-17).
- **Life 8** is where all four rewrote the same chapter (PP-23).

It **can** be one game, and the seed is already in the docs: the brief's 30-minute day, narrative's Routine layer, territory's "food is power outside, love inside", nemesis's R13 "two lives" rules (curfew taunts, Wall Watch, the Call heard by Rivals) and crew's Home Moves. What is missing is a **single owner for the day and for Life 8** who budgets every system's demands against 20 minutes of clock and two home slots. I recommend that owner be the creative director, through a short "Day & Life 8" section in the brief that overrides the GDDs.

## 12. Is the scope credible? The true minimum viable game

**No.** As written, the full vision is about 64 Moves, about 1,200 bark templates, about 113 vignettes (each Keystone in full, essential-cut and montage forms, evening ones in two stagings), 9 house states, 7 human life-stage sets, 6 districts with 30 hand-built Patches plus arena anchors, 6 authored Bosses plus a Usurper, about 120 tracked procedural cats whose Scars must read from above, 12 Friend Strays, 6 Elders, 5 Keepsakes, 5 story recruits, weather, Critters including a tanuki that plays dead, and four aged versions of Mikan. The team size is unknown (brief §11.2). Narrative's slice alone needs 16 vignettes against the brief's budget of 10. Any one of these four systems would make a whole indie game.

**The true minimum viable game** (it keeps every pillar and every never-cut item, and cuts the rest):

## 14. The changes that would most raise those scores

1. **Write the day (P2 +2, P3 +1).** A one-page brief section: 20 clock minutes, about 6 encounters, ≤ 3 minutes at the Lot, ≤ 3 daily-reset actions in the whole game, a Way Home of ≤ 90 s from anywhere. Then each GDD cuts to fit (PP-13, PP-17, PP-30).
2. **Two cats and a Pile-On by minute 12 (P3 +2).** A story Crew cat on Life 1 day 1, a second by the end of Life 1, and the first Pile-On with two cats in the dust cloud (PP-04, PP-09). Then prototype the MVP question with Mikan's cross-Style slots capped at 2 (PP-20).
3. **One Life 8 (P2 +1, P1 +1).** Narrative owns the stages and Territory, Nemesis and Crew supply numbers. One Carry a day, three Carries to restore the house, Reckoning pressure only after the first Carry, an optional Usurper, an indifferent Sniff (PP-23, PP-24, PP-25).
4. **Make home pay in the fight (P2 +1, P3 +0.5).** Replace +2%/heart Nerve with something felt in the first second of a fight: Well-Fed gives +1 Paw at encounter start, or the Zoomies gauge starts at 50%. Then an evening at home is visible in the next morning's first brawl (PP-19).
5. **Protect the Closing Keystones (P2 +1).** Closings never play in Forensic staging; on a Late arrival they wait for the next morning slot. This fixes the Life 4 proposal (PP-18).
6. **One Verdict per fight, one recruit rule (P4 +1).** The highest-ranked Rival only, with a deterministic ear-read threshold that Crew owns (PP-14, PP-21).
7. **The glossary pass (all pillars, indirectly).** `temperament`, the Style chart, the Move catalogue, and the Pounce, Hiss, Bunny-Kick, Settled, Lookout and Stubborn duplicates (PP-15, PP-22).
8. **Cut the spreadsheet (P1 +1).** No Rations, no visible Power numbers, no willingness percentage, no Clout points (milestones only). Ears, smell and fish piles carry it (territory OQ8 and OQ15).

**Projected after these changes:** P1 8, P2 8, P3 7 (this one must be proven in the prototype), P4 9.
