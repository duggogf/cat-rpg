# Preview Critique: ten mock-up frames

**Reviewer:** art-director · **Date:** 2026-11-24
**Against:** the user's style reference (Shin chan: Shiro and the Coal Town still), `design/art/art-bible.md` §1 (identity, line rule), §4 (palette), §5 (characters), §6 (environment), §7 (UI), §10 (combat VFX), and `design/gdd/camera-system.md` §3.6–3.7.

---

## Overall

**One game?** Mostly. The shared kit carries it: every frame uses the same cream-and-cocoa bean panels, the M PLUS Rounded type, Aubergine Ink cels with the cream rim, Bell-Gold for "ours" and Sage for the Wall Sitters. Two things break the set. The first is **character model drift between the artist groups**. Togemaru is a cream long-haired tabby in 02, 03, 04 and 08 and a black-and-tan cat in 05 and 06. Kagi is a grey tabby in 06 and 07 and a tuxedo in 08. The Posse is called Mochi and Gin in 02 and 03 but Pon and Hige in 08 and the GDD. The second is **two background registers**. The street frames (01, 02, 03, 09) have painted foliage and a sky. The home frame (10) is flat vector shapes with inked furniture and looks like a different game.

**Does it hit the reference?** On characters and UI, yes. The flat two-tone cels, thin even line, simple shapes and rounded cream-and-brown UI are the right family, and the shapes are our own (beans, chestnuts, poms), not copies. On backgrounds, not yet. The reference sells its "lush summer" with saturated foliage masses, deep shadow under eaves, raking sunlight and a long view into the hills. Our daytime street frames have one flat, pale, evenly lit ground plane that fills 40–50% of the screen, with almost no cast shadow and no light direction. The Dusk frames (05, 06) are the only ones with a real lighting idea, and even there the values are a single lavender wash. The biggest look gain across the whole set is **cast light and shadow on the ground, plus a richer mid-ground**.

**Would it excite the user?**
- **Combat:** yes. 02 and 04 have real action-anime energy: slash arc, Frazzle puff, lettered word, Pile-On cloud. With the fixes below they are the frames to lead with.
- **Nemesis system:** half. 06 (the Fence Line) is the strongest new idea in the set and immediately says "these cats have a pecking order and remember you". 05 (the Standoff) is the weakest: it plays as a calm chat on a wall, not a rival confrontation.
- **Territory:** 08 is dense but convincing as a management screen. The map does not yet show who owns what at a glance.
- **Wordless owner story:** no. 10 is the one frame that misses the brief. Its camera, staging, light and glowing notes all work against the quiet emotion it is meant to sell.

**Cat appeal across the set:** Mikan's front views (04, 09) are charming. Mikan's **back view, which the player sees most, is the weakest drawing in the set**. It is a boxy white tofu block with a cog-shaped orange sticker for a tail (01, 03, 05). Fix it once in the kit and four frames improve.

## Per frame

### 01 Exploration: 7/10, FIX
**Works:** The composition is right for the third-person camera. Mikan sits low-centre, the lane leads the eye to a sea horizon, and the sky takes about a third of the frame (E-6). The human is legs, a bag and shoes only (§5.5). The wall cat on the High Road, the gold Head Home thread, the Sage scent wisps over an inked stone-lantern Scent Post, and the inked bucket and pot against an unlined painted wall all apply "ink what acts, paint what stays" correctly. Scribble on the wall notice. The phase dial matches §7.2.

**Fix:**
1. **Mikan's back view (cat appeal).** Rebuild the body as a soft bean/chestnut that tapers into the haunches, with visible shoulder blades and two hind feet. Replace the cog-shaped tail with a round, scalloped **pom** (§5.1, a hero shape). From behind, the ginger cap, red collar line, white back and pom must read as one appealing silhouette. At the moment it reads as a box.
2. **Summer light on the ground.** The lane is a flat, pale, shadowless plane across the lower half. Add a hard direction to the Day sun: long Wisteria cast shadows from the wall, fence and human across the lane, a warm sunlit band where the light falls, and a Mikan contact shadow (E-4). Break the empty foreground with a few painted cracks, weeds and a drain cover so it has depth.
3. **Mid-ground richness.** The wall on the left is a flat lilac slab. Paint the plaster with sun-to-shade gradation and brush texture, add an overhanging foliage shadow, and push the far town and hill backdrop card so the lane has the reference's depth.

### 02 Combat clash: 7/10, FIX
**Works:** This frame sells the combat. Mikan's diagonal lunge, the Claw slash arc with its Ink keystroke, the Frazzled target (Magenta outline, spiral eyes, puffed silhouette that reads in solid black), the readable hand-lettered **FWOOMP!** tilted along the hit, radial focus lines with the centre clear, dust-puff rings, and the Sage name banner with the brick emblem all follow §10. The Posse stack and the Tag-In dial are clear.

**Fix:**
1. **Remove the camera roll** (−2.2° on the scene group); see the ruling below. Recover the energy with a stronger diagonal in Mikan's pose and a bigger, closer impact.
2. **Mikan's lunge (cat appeal).** The body is a stiff straight plank, and the hind legs trail as a single sausage. Draw a real stretch-and-reach: arched spine, splayed hind legs pushing off, tail pom flying, ears pinned forward. Move the bell tick up so it floats clear of the head instead of sitting on the ear like a hat.
3. **Ground and space.** The arena floor is a flat beige plane with no light. Add the Day cast shadows from the fence, a painted light pool over the fight ring, and value contrast between the clear 10 m ring and the cluttered edge (§6.2). The fight should sit in a place, not on a tabletop.

### 03 Combat Slow Blink: 8/10, PASS
**Works:** This is the clearest gameplay UI in the set. The Cocoa-header menu with the chestnut portrait, readable Move names, Paw costs and the gold selected row; L1/R1 cat switching; the gold lock brackets and Magenta-ringed Frazzled target; the frozen calico mid-leap showing world time at 0.05. The leader and the target are unmistakable. Pre-production notes, no further pass needed: the grade is too subtle to read in a still (see the ruling), and Mikan should hold the Scrapper stance rather than sit.

### 04 Combat Pile-On: 8/10, FIX
**Works:** This is the signature image and it already lands. The static Bell-Gold panel, full-field Ink lines, scalloped Cream cloud with a heavy line, coat-coloured paws and tails, stars, chevron ticks and bell, and a giant readable **KA-BLAM!** at about 30% of screen height all match §10.5. Mikan's front view is the most appealing drawing in the set.

**Fix:**
1. **Take the face off the cloud.** The blue eyes, brows and closed-eye mark make the cloud read as a sheep character. The cloud is a dust boil. Replace the face with more pop-outs: an ear, a bottle-brushed tail, a flying tuft.
2. **Commit the backing.** The bottom-right corner shows the ground, bucket and a halftone patch, which reads as an unfinished wipe. Either fill the panel edge to edge or, if the intent is the halftone entry wipe (§10.4), show it as a clean diagonal halftone band.
3. **Personality beats.** Each Posse cat should pop out with its personality flourish (§3.3): the Braggart on top striking a pose, the Goofball tumbling tail-first. At the moment the calico is asleep at the edge and the routed tabby on the right is the biggest figure. Make Mikan larger and central, and shrink the routed target into the blow-apart beat.

### 05 Rival Standoff: 6/10, FIX
**Works:** The Dusk key (lavender zenith, orange horizon, sun on the sea, warm windows) is the most atmospheric light in the set. The line of action is right: Mikan on the wall top from behind, the rival facing the camera. The bark balloon is an opaque Cream bean with a Sage speaker ribbon, and the line is readable and memory-citing. The notched ear shows the history on the model.

**Fix:**
1. **Make it a standoff (§10.5 and camera §3.7).** Both cats arch. Add speed-line bands behind each cat, a hard light wedge between them and, because Togemaru is the Nemesis, a Frazzle Magenta band behind him and the storm mark over his head. Push in so his face is at least 25% of screen height. At the moment Mikan is loafing and Togemaru is sitting upright: this reads as a chat.
2. **The name card.** Use one Standoff card design across the set: a wedge-ended band in the kit's palette (Cream ground and Cocoa frame, or the Clowder colour as 02 does), a chestnut portrait, a Title stamp and a Clowder ribbon, slammed into the upper third. Set the name in the UI rounded face, not the lettering stand-in (Dela Gothic One). A near-black Ink band is not in the UI palette, and it makes this frame look like a different game from 02.
3. **Togemaru's design and the values.** Resolve Togemaru to the one design used in 02, 03, 04 and 08 (see consistency). Lift the Dusk values: the roofs and the lane are one murky lavender mass. Add a warm rim of low sun on the wall top and on both cats, and darken the foreground bushes so the wall top reads as a lit stage.

### 06 Fence Line: 8/10, FIX
**Works:** A strong, original screen that sells the nemesis hierarchy at first glance. Rank is shown by height on one painted Lane wall with no rank labels (§7.6). Daifuku is a huge loaf at the top and a skyline landmark; Nori sits on the wire; the storm mark with a dashed line to Mikan is clear; Clowder-colour name ribbons carry Ink text. The memory lines on the card ("You notched my ear") make the Rival feel personal. The Dusk sky and lighthouse give it a place.

**Fix:**
1. **Selection must not read as allegiance.** Togemaru is selected with a Bell-Gold keyline, which is the Posse colour (§4.3). Keep his Sage outline and mark the selection with a pom cursor or a pulsing Cream rim plus the paw-pad cursor.
2. **Disposition poses (cat appeal).** Most cats are the same frontal upright sit with stick legs (Ohagi, Goma, Togemaru, Mikan). Give each cat its personality's disposition pose from §3.3: the Braggart on its highest surface flicking a paw, the Stalwart square-on, a Bitter cat under a grumble cloud, a sleeper loafed. The wall should look like a crowd of characters, not a line-up.
3. **Bottom-right clutter.** The card overlaps the wall base, and the unnamed pair beside Mikan crowds the "Card / Back" buttons. Move the card left or shorten it, give the bottom-right pair names or remove them, and stop the inked plank fence on the right (environment set dressing is painted, L-1).

### 07 Verdict: 7/10, PASS
**Works:** The four-direction verdict layout is immediately understandable: Chase Off, Nose-Touch, Humble and Recruit, each with a consequence line, and a Bribe row in the only place numbers are allowed (§7.4). The defeated Captain is dazed with spiral eyes and puffs. The Sage ribbon names him. The shopfront is painted and unlined, with Scribble on the sign and noren. Presentable. Before the next pass, fix Mikan's pom tail, which is drawn in front of the calico and reads as hers (consistency list), and give the ground a cast shadow.

### 08 Turf Board: 7/10, FIX
**Works:** The plank-fence frame with Crew cards pinned by clothes-pegs is the diegetic idea from §6.4, done well. The painted Lane map with sea, Scent Posts, wisps and patrol paths is rich. The right-hand panel reads cleanly, Post Hige uses Collar Crimson with Cream text correctly, and numbers stay on this board as the rules allow. It looks like a game you would manage.

**Fix:**
1. **Rule break: "♥ +1".** A number is measuring a bond (§7.4: numbers never measure a relationship). Show the heart alone, or a heart that pops in two steps.
2. **Ownership at a glance.** Tint each Patch on the map in its owner's colour: a Bell-Gold wash with a cream edge for yours, Sage for the Wall Sitters, and a dashed edge for contested ones. At the moment you have to read the shields on every label to see what you hold, and territory is the headline here.
3. **One input set and less label cover.** The tabs use gamepad glyphs (L1/R1) while the footer uses keyboard keys (S, N, W, V, E). Show one device per mock (the other frames are gamepad). The five Patch labels cover about 30% of the map. Shrink them to a pom plus the scent pips and number, and give the full label only to the selected Patch.

### 09 Tribute Doorstep: 7/10, PASS
**Works:** It is clear and charming: Mikan on the step, the Posse arriving with fish in their mouths, the Tribute pile, Sniff tallies in fish units only, and the raid warning with the rival's chestnut portrait. Haru's yellow appears only as laundry (§4.5). Hydrangeas, the post box, the roof and the plaster wall are painted and unlined. Presentable. Future polish: §2 asks for the Morning key, with the Tribute pile catching the first low sun. This is neutral Day light. Unify Mikan's tail (consistency list).

### 10 Home evening: 5/10, FIX
**Works:** The story idea is the right one: Haru (yellow, with yellow socks below 60 cm) at the upright piano, Mikan walking on the inked keys, Gran present only as plum legs with the cross-hatch motif and plum slippers, the school bag with the ginger-cat charm as the daily find, Scribble sheet music, and no faces. Mikan's ginger cap is in frame (§1.4). The Dusk window colour is correct.

**Fix:**
1. **Camera and staging at cat height, built around the bond.** The camera sits around 1 m and looks across the keys, and Haru's back is an undefined yellow block covering 35% of the frame. Use the Home camera (0.3–0.6 m; §6.3), either along the keyboard at key level or from the floor beside the bench. Bring Mikan up to about 30% of the frame, stopped on the keys looking up, with Haru's hand lifting off the keys palm-down toward Mikan (§5.5: "hands are the faces"). The emotion is the pause in the music, not the music.
2. **Remove the glowing notes; use light instead.** Additive glowing musical notes are VFX and glow, which the house never gets (P4, P5). Carry the feeling with light. Raking Dusk light from the window lies across the keys and Mikan, warm and soft. The rest of the room is cool and quiet. Haru's study-lamp pool can be a second warm note.
3. **Paint the room.** The bench, the piano body and the floorboards are flat vector shapes with Ink outlines. Only the piano lid and keys may be inked (L-1). Repaint the bench, the piano case, the floor and the shoji as soft, unlined, brush-textured surfaces with a painted terminator (E-1, E-2), so the house looks "painted like a memory", as the street frames already do.

## Rulings on the two artist questions

### Frame 02: the slight dutch tilt
**Ruling: no tilt in gameplay frames. Remove the −2.2° roll.**
Camera §3.6 says shake is screen-space translation with **no roll**, and §3.7 allows tilt **only behind a full graphic panel** (the Zoomies cut-in, the T5 flat panel). Frame 02 is a live T4 gameplay frame. A mock that shows a tilt promises the user something the camera will never do, and a rolled horizon in live play costs readability and comfort. The energy belongs in the drawings that are allowed to tilt: the lettering (8–15°, already used), the radial lines, the slash arc and Mikan's diagonal pose. **Allowed:** a tilt inside frame 04's Pile-On panel or a Zoomies cut-in, if an artist wants it.

### Frame 03: leader and target in full colour under the Slow Blink grade
**Ruling: approved, with limits.** The Slow Blink grade (saturation −30%, value −10%; Art owns it, camera §3.5) exists so the player can choose, and keeping the two things being chosen between at full colour is exactly the job. It also matches §10.1 rule 5: the controlled cat is never covered. Limits:
1. Only the **controlled cat** and the **current target** stay ungraded, together with the HUD, the menu, the lock brackets and the telegraph decals (gameplay information, §10.6). Every other cat, Posse included, takes the grade, so the exemption keeps its meaning. Moving the cursor moves the exemption.
2. The grade must be **visible**. In the current still, the world looks almost unchanged. Apply the full −30% / −10% with the cool lavender cast. If the effect needs more help, use a soft **cream** edge vignette, never a dark one (§4.4).
3. Recommended: add this exemption to the art bible's Slow Blink grade entry in the next art-bible pass (with the user's approval), so it becomes canon rather than a mock-up convention.

## Consistency list (must match across all frames)

1. **Togemaru's design.** Cream long-haired tabby (02, 03, 04, 08) against a black-and-tan cat with a spiky ruff (05, 06). Pick one and redraw the others. He is the arc lead and the user will look for him. **Story state:** 05 and 06 show him as the declared Nemesis, while 08 shows him in the Posse. The GDD says a declared Nemesis can't be Recruited in the slice, so the set should show one branch or label 08 as the other one.
2. **Kagi's design.** Grey tabby (06, 07) against a tuxedo (08).
3. **Posse names.** "Mochi" and "Gin" (02, 03) are not canon. Use the GDD crew (Pon, Hige, Togemaru, Kagi, Kinako) everywhere, and give each cat one coat across frames (e.g. Pon is the calico).
4. **Mikan's tail.** A cog/star-shaped orange sticker that moves around: off the hip in 01, 03 and 05, floating beside the body in 07 and 09, on the back in 10. Draw one round, scalloped pom, always attached at the base of the spine.
5. **Mikan's eye patch.** §5.1 puts a ginger patch over the left eye. It is clear in 04 and missing or ambiguous in the front views in 06, 07 and 09 and the portraits.
6. **Mikan's body.** Rounded and appealing in 04 and 09, boxy in 01, 05 and 07, a stiff plank in 02. Use one model sheet.
7. **Controlled-cat bell tick.** It floats clear above the head in 03 and 04, but sits on the ear in 02 and is missing in 01 (acceptable in Explore if that is the rule: state it).
8. **Standoff name card.** Sage band with the brick emblem (02, 03), an Ink band with a Sage underline and a chestnut portrait (05, 06), and a flat Sage ribbon (07). Use one wedge-ended card design for the Standoff and the combat banner, and one ribbon for nameplates.
9. **Fonts.** 05 and 06 set names in Dela Gothic One, the kit's stand-in for **combat lettering**. §7.4 allows one rounded UI family. Keep Dela Gothic One for sound words only.
10. **Posse HUD.** Four bean cards with bars and paw pips (02, 03) against three chestnut portraits with bars (05). If Explore and Combat really have different HUDs, keep the same portrait crop and bar style in both.
11. **Input glyphs.** Gamepad in 02, 03, 05, 06, 07 and 09; keyboard keys mixed into 08's footer. One device per set.
12. **Line weight.** The cels hold a thin, even line, but 10's furniture and floor carry the character Ink line, and 04's cloud line is correctly heavier as a set-piece exception. Nothing else should be inked: environments are painted in every frame.
13. **Background register.** 10 is flat vector shapes and the street frames are painted. Every frame needs the same painted surfaces, phase-coloured shadows and brush texture.
