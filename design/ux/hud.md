## 1. HUD Philosophy

**What is this game's relationship with on-screen information?**

There are three HUDs because there are three ways of being a cat. **On the street, the world is the interface**: a cat reads scent, light and posture, so territory, time and threat are carried by Whisker Sense, the sky and animation, and the screen stays almost empty. **In a brawl, the HUD is a conductor's score**: the player directs four cats at once, so the HUD is allowed to be loud, dense and fully explicit, and it must stay legible when the screen is shaking and full of dust. **At home, there is no interface at all**: the house is the one place where the player is not a boss reading gauges but an animal reading people, and any panel, label or number would break that. The HUD's density is therefore itself a signal of which life the player is in (Pillar 2), and its disappearance at the garden wall is a designed beat, not a side effect.

**Visibility principle**: **CONTEXTUAL, resolving to HIDE** outside combat and **SHOW** inside combat. When in doubt on the street or at home, hide it and put it behind Whisker Sense ("hold to know"). When in doubt in a fight, show it.

**The Rule of Necessity for this game**:

"A HUD element earns its place when (a) a cat could perceive the thing it reports, and (b) the player would make a demonstrably worse decision in the next five seconds without it, and (c) it cannot be carried by the world, by Whisker Sense, or by animation and sound with a visual twin."

Two hard vetoes sit above this rule and cannot be traded:

1. **Language veto (Pillar 1, brief §5.5).** No HUD element, caption, tooltip, accessibility aid or notification may render, translate, paraphrase or summarise a human utterance or human writing. This includes Haru's dev-facing name, which never appears as text in the HUD.
2. **Home veto (Pillar 2).** Inside the wall of Haru's House, no panels, bars, numbers, clocks or system text. Pictograms only.

Rationale: command input bottom-left and party status bottom-right follows the convention our target player already knows from the combat reference, so the mental model transfers. The enemy plate is top-centre and screen-anchored (not floating over the enemy) so it does not move with screen shake or get buried in VFX. The camera has an authored yaw and pitch of about 55 degrees, so the far side of the fight ring sits in the upper half of the screen: the bottom corners are the cheapest pixels in the frame.

### 4.2 Element Detail Blocks

---

**E1 — Sky Disc (day-phase clock)**

- Visual description: a small round badge. A sun or moon pictogram travels a shallow arc divided into four notched segments: Morning, Day, Dusk, Night. The current segment carries the phase pictogram (rising sun / high sun / low sun on a roofline / moon). A bowl pip sits on the Dusk segment once the evening bowl exists in the player's experience (teaches the curfew trade without words).
- Data displayed: phase and progress within it. **No digits, no minutes.** The day is a sky, not a countdown.
- Update behaviour: fades in 300 ms on phase change and holds 4 s; reappears for the last 60 s of each phase; shown while Whisker Sense is held; hidden otherwise. A setting makes it persistent.
- Urgency states: Normal; "phase ending" (gentle sway of the sun, no flashing); "Night ending" hands over to the Drowsy Vignette. There is no red state: nothing is failed by time.
- Clock paused: when the clock is paused but the HUD is visible (e.g. inside a fight ring before the HUD swaps), the disc is hidden rather than shown frozen.
- Interaction: display only.
- Player customisation: Contextual (default) / Always / Off.

---

**E2 — Posse Strip (exploration)**

- Visual description: up to four round portrait chips in a row, bottom-right. Each chip has a thin Nerve arc around it. A chip with a full Zoomies gauge gains a jagged crown ring.
- Data displayed: who is in the Posse, approximate Nerve, Routed state (chip greyed with a dust-puff pictogram), Zoomies-ready, Well-Fed bowl pip at day start.
- Update behaviour: appears only when something is not nominal (anyone hurt or Routed, Zoomies ready), for 5 s after a fight, or while Whisker Sense is held (then with numbers). Otherwise hidden.
- Interaction: display only. Posse changes happen via pause (see `key-screens.md`).

---

**E3 — Context Prompt**

- Visual description: one button glyph in a round cream token with a short verb pill ("Spray", "Sniff", "Nap", "Beg", "Post Sentry"), floating above the interactable. Only one prompt at a time; nearest and most-facing wins.
- Urgency states: none. Naps show a small half-phase mark on the pill because a Sunspot nap costs half a phase (canon) — consequence visible before commitment.
- Interaction: North / E. Hold-type prompts draw a filling ring; a setting converts every hold to press-then-confirm.

---

**E4 — Patch Tag**

- Visual description: a pill with the holder's Clowder emblem on a patterned disc, the Patch name, and a ring gauge for Scent with the number inside. A crown-cushion pip marks the Den. A small cat-head pip marks a posted Sentry.
- Data displayed: Patch name, holder Clowder, Scent 0–100, Den flag, Sentry posted yes/no.
- Update behaviour: slides in when a Patch border is crossed and holds 3 s; reappears within 4 m of the Scent Post. After Spraying, the ring fills live with the sparkle cloud, then the tag retires.
- Notes: Patch names are system text in the cat's terms (place names), never readable human signage.

---

**E5 — Whisker Sense layer**

Canon: hold-button sense mode; the world desaturates; scent glows in each Clowder's colour; footprints, hidden food and High Road routes are revealed. This is the game's map, objective marker and resource readout in one diegetic gesture.

- World-space content while active:
  - **Patch borders**: a line of scent wisps on the ground along each border, in the holder's colour and particle shape. Opacity and density scale with Scent; at low Scent the line breaks into dashes.
  - **Scent Posts**: a column of wisps over each post in view; height scales with Scent; the Patch Tag attaches to the nearest.
  - **Trails and footprints**: Rival footprints as paw prints with a direction taper; a pinned Rival's prints (see Fence Line spec) carry that Rival's portrait chip at the freshest end.
  - **Hidden food**: small steam curls.
  - **High Road routes**: faint bell-gold paw dots on reachable ledges within about 15 m.
  - **Home thread**: one soft cream wisp line leading toward Haru's House, always present. It is a smell, not an arrow: it follows streets, thins with distance and never shows a distance number.
  - **Sentries**: a posted Crew cat's own bell-gold marker over its Patch.
- Screen-space additions while active: Sky Disc, Posse Strip with numbers, and a small carried-Morsels chip under the Sky Disc. Nothing else.
- Update behaviour: 150 ms in, 250 ms out. Movement is allowed while active (assumption).
- Interaction: L2 / Q, hold by default; toggle option.
- Not available: in combat (L2 is reassigned), at home (it would add reading aids to the house; Mikan may still sniff objects via H1).

---

**E6 — Call Ripple (the pull to go home)**

- Visual description: three small note-dots on a short arc at the screen edge on the bearing of home, drawn at the three pitches of the Call (rise a third, fall a fifth) so the contour itself is recognisable. They appear in the rhythm of the motif.
- Data displayed: "the Call was sung, from that way." No text, no distance, no timer.
- Update behaviour: plays when the Call sounds at Dusk; repeats softly at a tunable interval through Dusk; stops the moment Mikan is inside the wall or the phase ends. Never shown at Night (nobody is calling: that is the waiting-up trade).
- Colour: the singer's signature colour, with a dark outline and the contour shape as the non-colour carrier. Proposal for Narrative: yellow for Haru; **no ripple at all in Life 8**; coral in Life 9 when Koharu sings. The HUD going silent at Dusk in Life 8 is intended to be felt.
- Haptic: three pulses matching the motif.
- Interaction: none. It is never a waypoint and never clickable.

---

**E7 — Caterwaul Flare** · **E9 — Sting Flare**

- Caterwaul: an edge chip with a yowling-mouth pictogram and the Patch name, on the bearing of the Patch; persists while live defence is still possible; clears with a small "too late" or "defended" pictogram. Appears in combat too, but collapsed to the pictogram only.
- Sting Flare: when a Rival's personal yowl-sting plays from off-screen (canon: before an Ambush), an edge chip shows that Rival's portrait (silhouette if unknown) on the bearing of the sound for 2 s. This is the visual twin of an audio-only warning and is on for all players, not only in an accessibility mode.

---

**E8 — Awareness Marks and Ambush Prompt**

- Marks above non-hostile-yet cats, shape-coded: closed-eye crescent (unaware), single ear-up triangle (suspicious), double-ear spike (alerted). Shown only while Stalking, while on the High Road above them, or within sight range.
- Ambush Prompt: when an Ambush is valid, the attack glyph appears over the target with a downward chevron and the word "Ambush". Communicates the Ruffled opener before commitment.

---

**C1 — Posse Panel**

- Visual description: four stacked rows in one cream panel, bottom-right. The leader's row is taller, sits at the top of the stack and carries the bell mark. Each row: portrait chip (with Zoomies ring), name, Nerve bar with current/max numerals, Paw pads, status icons.
- **Paws**: drawn as paw pads. Empty pad = outline; filling pad = fills bottom-to-top; full pad = solid with a toe-bean highlight and a one-time pop. Mikan shows 2 pads (3 from Life 4); Crew show 2.
- **Conductor cue**: when an AI-controlled cat has at least 1 full Paw, its row shows the ally-command glyph (L2 or R2 / Q or E) next to the pads. This is the single most important teaching element in the HUD: it says "someone is waiting for you" without a pop-up.
- **Zoomies**: a ring around the portrait chip, filling clockwise; when full the ring becomes jagged and the row shows a small "Zoomies" label.
- Urgency states per row: Normal; Low (below 25%: bar notch icon and the numerals bold); Routed (row dims, dust-puff pictogram, pads empty); Leader (bell mark, enlarged).
- Update behaviour: Nerve lerps over 150 ms with a lagging "chip" segment showing the size of the last hit. The panel is **not** attached to camera shake.
- Posse of fewer than four (Life 1 onward): the panel shows only the rows that exist. In the first fight it is one row.
- Interaction: display only (mouse: clicking a row switches leader).
- Player customisation: opacity, size S/M/L, numerals on/off, mirrored layout (swap BL and BR).

---

**C2 — Command Stub and Slow Blink Command Menu**

Real time: a three-line stub bottom-left showing the glyphs for Attack, Dodge and Slow Blink, plus Guard and Shortcut hints in small type. It fades to 40% opacity after the player's fifth fight and can be turned off.

Slow Blink (time scale 5%, canon): the stub unfolds into the command menu on the frame of the press (Pillar 3 engineering note). The camera pulls in; the world dims slightly behind an eyelid-shaped vignette (top and bottom soften, like half-closed eyes).

- Structure: **flat, one level.** A cat has only 4 Moves, so there is no "Abilities" submenu. Entries for the focused cat, in fixed order: Move 1–4, then **Zoomies** (present only when full), then **Pile-On** (present only when an enemy is Frazzled; pinned to the top while present). Minimum path: open (1) → choose (1) → confirm target (1) = three presses.
- Each Move entry shows: name, Paw cost as pads plus a numeral, damage-type glyph (Claw / Fang / Thump / Yowl), and a target-shape pip (single / line / ring / ally / self). Unaffordable Moves are dimmed with the missing pads outlined; they stay selectable for inspection but cannot be confirmed.
- Cat tabs: four portrait tabs along the top edge of the menu; the focused cat's tab is raised. L2/R2 (Q/E) cycle. The world camera eases to frame the focused cat.
- Cursor memory: per cat, per fight. First open of a fight lands on Move 1 (or Pile-On if present).
- **Fear highlight**: if the current target has a known Fear or weakness that a Move would hit, that entry carries a "Fear!" tag and a chevron. If a prop in the ring could hit a known Fear, the prop glints in the world. Unknown Fears show nothing.
- Target select step: after choosing a Move, the menu collapses to a one-line header ("Mikan · [Move name] · cost") and the reticle takes over. Left/right cycles valid targets in screen order; the Target Plate follows. For ring/line Moves the player moves a ground shape. For ally Moves the Posse Panel rows become the list. A forecast strip under the Target Plate shows qualitative outcome only: weak / resisted / hits a Fear / will Frazzle. No predicted damage numbers (feel beats simulation).
- After confirming: Slow Blink ends immediately by default (pace). Option: "Chain commands" keeps it open while any Posse cat can still afford a Move.
- Inspect: holding North / F on an entry shows a two-line description card.
- Slow Blink has no time limit and no cost (assumption).

---

**C3 — Shortcut Diamond**

- While L1 is held, a diamond of the leader's four Moves appears bottom-left, mapped to West / North / East / South in the same order the Slow Blink menu lists them (1–4). Pressing a face button fires that Move at the current target with **no slow-mo**. Keyboard: keys 1–4 fire directly with no modifier.
- The diamond also works inside Slow Blink (direct pick).
- Order is fixed by slot, so the mapping never changes when Moves become unaffordable. Slot order is set by the player in the Crew screens.
- Hold is default; a toggle option exists (one-handed play).

---

**C4 — Target Plate** · **C5 — Boss Plate**

- Target Plate rows: (1) Clowder emblem disc, name, Title, rank pip (Tough / Captain / Boss / Great Boss as 1–4 stacked ear-notches), level; (2) Nerve bar; (3) Frazzle gauge with a text state label at its right end: blank / "RUFFLED" / "FRAZZLED"; (4) up to three known Fear and Strength icons (Fear = icon in a downward-pointing shield, Strength = icon in an upward chevron frame), unknown ones as "?" only once the Rival card has revealed the slot exists.
- Frazzle states: Normal fill; **Ruffled** = hatched fill travelling along the gauge plus the label (gain x2, canon); **Frazzled** = gauge becomes an 8-second countdown bar draining right-to-left with the label, and the Pile-On Prompt appears. The damage multiplier (x1.5 rising to x2.0) is shown as a small "x1.5" numeral ticking up next to the label.
- Strays and Critters: name only, no Title row; plate is one row shorter.
- Boss Plate: same content, wider, with the Nerve bar segmented into phases if Combat defines phases. Used for Bosses and Captains. In a duo fight (Kiha & Moha) two half-width plates sit side by side.
- Only one plate at a time. Other enemies use overhead mini-bars (C6).

---

**C6 — World Markers**

- **Leader marker**: a bell pictogram on a short stalk above the controlled cat plus a bell-gold ground ring. Always on top of VFX (rendered after effects, see §7). The canon bell-gold outline tick marks all four Posse cats; the leader marker is what separates "mine" from "ours".
- **Bell ping**: L3 / G plays a bell jingle and pulses a large ring on the leader for 0.5 s. Available at any time. This is the answer to "I lost my cat."
- **Reticle**: a ring with four whisker ticks on the current target; solid when locked, dashed when soft-targeted. Enemy outline thickens on the target.
- **Overhead mini-bars**: a short Nerve bar and a thinner Frazzle bar, shown only over (a) the current target, (b) enemies damaged in the last 3 s, (c) all enemies during Slow Blink. Hidden otherwise.
- **Off-screen arrows**: bell-gold chevrons with portrait chips for Posse cats outside the frame; Clowder-emblem chevrons for enemies outside the frame only while they are winding up a Move.
- **Tells**: any telegraphed enemy Move draws a ground shape (ring, cone, line) with a hatched fill that tightens with the wind-up, and an enemy Move ribbon (C9). Shape and motion carry the tell, never hue alone.

---

**C7 — Damage Numbers**

Rules, in priority order:

1. **Whose numbers show.** Always: the leader's hits, every Move by anyone, all healing, all damage to Posse cats. Not shown: basic attacks by AI-controlled Crew (their Frazzle and Nerve effect is visible on the bars). This halves number count in a four-cat brawl.
2. **Merging.** Multi-hit strings merge into one rolling number per attacker-target pair within a 600 ms window; the number counts up in place instead of spawning new ones.
3. **Budget.** At most 6 numbers on screen; the oldest basic-hit number is evicted first. Move numbers are never evicted by basic-hit numbers.
4. **Form over colour.** Dealt = plain numeral. Weak / Fear hit = larger with a double chevron above. Resisted = smaller inside a rounded shield notch. On a Frazzled target = inside a puff-burst frame. Heal = "+" prefix in a rounded leaf frame. Taken by a Posse cat = "-" prefix, shown small over the cat and mirrored by the lagging chip on its Nerve bar.
5. **Placement.** World-anchored at the hit point, popped up-and-outward away from the leader, then rendered on the HUD layer so they are never occluded by geometry or dust clouds. They do not inherit camera shake.
6. **Scale.** Canon damage runs from about 8 to a displayed cap of 9,999. Size steps come from the hit's class (basic / 1-Paw / 2-Paw / Zoomies), not from the number's magnitude, so late-game hits are not all huge.
7. **Slow Blink.** Numbers in flight freeze and dim to 50% so the menu reads.
8. **Options.** All / Moves only / Off.

---

**C8 — Glyph Bursts (onomatopoeia)**

Canon: onomatopoeia is rendered as paw-print and claw-slash glyph bursts, not readable words.

1. **Never letterforms.** No Latin, kana, kanji, numerals or anything that can be sounded out. Glyphs must also be visibly distinct from **Scribble** (the human-writing glyph set) so the player never reads a hit effect as human text.
2. **Shape encodes damage type** (a redundant, colour-free channel): Claw = three parallel slash marks; Fang = a two-point bite crescent; Thump = a paw print in a star burst; Yowl = concentric wavy arcs. Fear hits add a jagged outer ring. Art-director owns the drawing; UX requires the four to be distinguishable in silhouette at 48 px.
3. **Budget.** One large burst at a time (matching the canon "one screen-wide effect at a time"); up to three small bursts. Large bursts are reserved for Moves, Frazzle breaks, Pile-On and Zoomies, and are synchronised to hit-stop.
4. **Placement.** Bursts are world-anchored and drawn behind the leader marker, reticle and damage numbers. They may not cover the bottom corner panels or the Target Plate.
5. **Options.** Intensity slider (100% / 50% / minimal). At "minimal", bursts become a single small type glyph at the hit point.

---

**C9 — Move Callout Ribbons**

- Ally: when any Posse cat starts a Move, a small ribbon over that cat shows the Move name and damage-type glyph for 1.2 s.
- Enemy: when an enemy starts a telegraphed Move, a ribbon in the enemy's outline colour and emblem shows the Move name above it, and the same name appears as a one-line callout beneath the Target Plate if that enemy is the target. Cat-to-cat information: allowed as readable text.
- At most one enemy callout and two ally ribbons at once; newer replaces older.

---

**C10 — Pile-On Prompt**

- When any enemy is Frazzled: a large contextual glyph (North / F) with the word "Pile-On" appears over the Frazzled enemy with a dust-puff frame; the same entry is pinned to the top of the Slow Blink menu; the Frazzle countdown is visible on the plate; controller pulses once; a distinct sound plays.
- If more than one enemy is Frazzled the prompt sits on the current target and cycles with target change.
- During the Pile-On itself: HUD hides except the Posse Panel (dimmed) — the dust cloud is the signature image and gets the whole screen.

---

**C11 — Standoff Name-Card**

- A lower-third card for 2–4 s (canon): portrait with Scars, name in large type, Title beneath, rank pip, Clowder emblem, and the taunt line (60 characters or fewer) in a speech balloon tail-anchored to the Rival. A small hackles meter (five steps) shows Grudge tier; a spiked frame marks a Nemesis.
- Skippable after first sight of that Rival (South / Space). Accessibility: "Hold Standoffs until I press" option so slow readers are never cut off. On first sight it is not skippable but still honours the hold option.
- For Ambush openers there is no Standoff; the name and Title appear on the Target Plate with a 1 s highlight instead.

---

**C12 — Encounter Tally** · **C13 — Low-Nerve Warning**

- Tally: one card above the Posse Panel for 2.5 s: Morsels, Shinies if any, XP as a single bar tick per cat, level-up pips. If a Verdict is pending the tally waits until after it.
- Low Nerve: the leader's row shows a notch icon and bold numerals; screen corners take a soft static tint. No pulsing by default (a slow pulse is opt-in), no heartbeat audio without a visual twin.

---

**H1–H5 — Home elements**

- **H1 Pictogram Prompt**: a button glyph with a pictogram (lap, bowl, paw-knocking-object, leg-rub, window) and **no verb text**. Appears only within reach and only for the nearest interaction.
- **H2 Sense Icons**: canon. Tiny pictogram bubbles over Mikan showing what a cat grasps. Vocabulary is Narrative's; UX requires each icon to be shape-distinct, to hold at least 2 s, and never to contain letters or Scribble.
- **H3 Bond Heart Pop**: when Bond rises, one heart outline appears above Mikan and fills, then fades. No count, no "+1". The count (hearts only) lives in the pause menu.
- **H4 Sleep Ring**: at the sleeping spot, a hold ring with a crescent pictogram. Sleeping ends the day at any time (canon), so it is a hold (or press-then-confirm) to prevent accidents.
- **H5 Murmur Ribbons / Sound Cues**: optional, non-verbal. See §10.4.

Not present at home, by rule: Sky Disc, Posse Strip, Patch Tag, Whisker Sense, pickup ticks, hint cards with text, damage numbers, any name label over a human, any caption of Murmur.

**Readability under heavy VFX and screen shake — the rules**

1. **The HUD does not shake.** Camera shake applies to the world camera only. Screen-anchored HUD is exempt. The Posse Panel may take a positional bump of at most 3 px for 80 ms when the leader takes a heavy hit; this is disabled by the reduced-motion setting.
2. **Draw order** (back to front): world → VFX and dust → glyph bursts → enemy outlines and tells → overhead bars and reticle → leader marker → damage numbers → screen-anchored panels. A full-screen effect may never draw over the last four.
3. **Hit-stop freezes the world, not the HUD.** Bars keep animating through hit-stop so a frozen frame still reads as "the game is responding".
4. **Impact frames** (canon: manga impact frames) are full-screen for at most 2 frames and must leave the two bottom corner panels and the Target Plate un-inverted.
5. **Outlines are the identity channel**: bell-gold tick for the Posse, Clowder colour for enemies (canon). UX adds a required non-colour twin: the Clowder emblem pip on the overhead bar and plate, and the bell marker for the leader.
6. **Similar silhouettes**: on switching leader, the new leader gets the bell ping automatically and the camera eases over 150 ms instead of cutting.
7. **Dust cloud occlusion**: during a Pile-On the cloud is allowed to hide the cats; that is the joke. It is not allowed to hide another enemy's tell: tells render above the cloud.
8. **Handheld**: at 1280x720, drop overhead mini-bars for non-target enemies outside Slow Blink before shrinking any text.

### 10.4 Subtitles Specification

This game inverts the usual rule, so the specification is in two halves.

**Cat speech (readable by canon)** — this is the game's subtitle system.

- **Default setting**: always on; cannot be turned off, because cat lines have no voiced words and the balloon is the only carrier.
- **Position**: world-anchored balloon over the speaker with a tail; clamped inside the safe area; in combat, barks from off-screen cats dock to the Edge Ring with a portrait chip.
- **Max characters per line**: 30; **max lines**: 2 (canon caps barks and taunts at 60 characters).
- **Speaker identification**: balloon tail plus a name pill (name and Title for Rivals); never colour alone.
- **Background**: opaque cream balloon, dark outline.
- **Font size minimum**: 28 px at 1080p; scales with the text setting.
- **Persistence**: at least 2 s plus 60 ms per character, or until the vocalisation ends, whichever is longer; option to extend by 50% or 100%. Standoff taunts can be held until pressed.
- **Combat budget**: at most one bark balloon at a time during real-time combat; none during Slow Blink menu navigation.

**Human speech (Murmur) and writing (Scribble)** — never subtitled, never captioned as text, in any mode, including accessibility modes, trailers and screenshots. This is the language veto.

**Non-verbal twins for players who cannot hear** (proposed; needs creative-director and accessibility-specialist rulings — see Open Questions):

- **Murmur Ribbons** (optional, off by default): a wordless ribbon rising from a speaking human, in that human's signature colour and a per-human pattern. Its *shape and motion* carry what a cat would get from tone of voice: round and slow (warm), bouncing (laughter), jagged and fast (sharp), thin and sinking (sad), trembling (afraid). No glyphs, no Scribble, no words. It conveys tone, which a cat perceives, and not meaning, which a cat does not.
- **The Call**: always has the Call Ripple and the three-pulse haptic, for every player.
- **Music Motes** (optional): when a human's leitmotif instrument is playing, a few motes in their signature colour and pattern drift from them, or from the direction of the house when they are absent. This is the visual twin of "the music is about this person now". It must be authored per vignette by Narrative and Audio, not automated.
- **Sound Cues** (optional): for non-speech sounds a cat reacts to (can opening, key in the door, rain starting, phone ringing, footsteps on the stairs), an ear-shaped chip with a pictogram and a bearing, on the Edge Ring. Pictograms by default; a text-label variant is an open question because it adds system words to the home.
- **Street audio**: every gameplay-relevant sound already has a visual twin in this spec (Caterwaul Flare, Sting Flare, Paw pop, Pile-On prompt, tells).
